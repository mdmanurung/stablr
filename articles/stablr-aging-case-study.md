# Case study: age-associated cell types across eight cohorts

> **Summary.** We apply the `stablr` environment-stability workflow to a
> public healthy-aging PBMC atlas (2,609 donors, eight scRNA-seq
> cohorts). The workflow selects 24 of 60 donor-level features whose
> association with age replicates in every cohort under a random-forest
> learner with model-X knockoff decoys. Seven of eight cell-type trends
> predeclared by the source publication pass the selection bar, all with
> sign-consistent correlations (exact sign-test p = 0.0078).
> Leave-one-cohort-out ridge models predict age with mean absolute error
> between 6.7 and 17.7 years depending on the held-out cohort. This
> vignette is an application case study. It is not calibration evidence
> for the method; the calibration evidence is simulation-based and cited
> in the method documentation.

## 1 Background and aims

A biomarker panel is useful only if it transports. A feature that tracks
age in one cohort may fail in the next because of cohort-specific
composition, preprocessing, or chance. The environment-stability
workflow in `stablr` addresses this by fitting the selection procedure
separately in each environment and keeping only features that pass the
bar in all of them.

This case study asks two questions of a real aging atlas:

1.  Which donor-level cell-type features associate with age in the same
    direction, and with replicable selection frequency, in all eight
    cohorts?
2.  Do the selected features predict age in a cohort that the model
    never saw?

The analysis ran once with a fixed seed. The full script is
`inst/benchmarks/ws8_aging_showcase.R` in the package source; the
figures and statistics tables are regenerated by
`inst/benchmarks/ws8_figures.R`. The pipeline takes about 15 minutes, so
this vignette presents the saved results instead of recomputing them.

## 2 Data

### 2.1 Source and availability

The data come from a public healthy-aging PBMC atlas of eight scRNA-seq
cohorts (Terekhova et al. 2026, *Immunity*), distributed through the
Artyomov lab single-cell portal (dataset
`pan_atlas_healthy_aging_all_celtypes`). We downloaded the per-cell
annotation table (`all_cells_metadata.csv.gz`, 13,137,776 cells; MD5
`50651968855bf6ca3efcdf199e2a9fd5`; retrieved 2026-10-13). The table
carries one row per cell with donor id, age, sex, CMV status, ancestry,
cohort, and cell-type annotation.

The cohorts differ in size (33 to 981 donors), ancestry mix, and age
range. Cohort identity is the largest variance component of the atlas
(Terekhova et al. 2026), which makes it the right stress test for
transportability.

### 2.3 Donor-level feature construction

Each donor is one sample. For each donor and cell type c we compute the
add-half smoothed proportion \tilde p_c = (n_c + 0.5)/(N + 0.5), where
n_c is the donor’s cell count for type c and N the donor’s total typed
cells, and use the logit transform \log\\\tilde p_c/(1-\tilde p_c)\\ as
the feature. This gives 59 cell-type features. One covariate (sex, coded
Female = 1) joins the panel. Three covariate columns (CMV status and two
ancestry indicators) are constant inside at least one cohort, so the
analysis drops them: a column that is constant inside a cohort has no
within-cohort signal, and its zero variance makes the equicorrelated
knockoff construction numerically infeasible. No cell-type feature is
constant in any cohort. The final panel has 60 features. The outcome is
donor age in years; the environments are the eight cohorts.

## 3 Statistical methods

### 3.1 Selection procedure

For each cohort separately,
[`stabl_environment_stability()`](https://mdmanurung.github.io/stablr/reference/stabl_environment_stability.md)
runs the STABL procedure (Hédou et al. 2024): 200 bootstrap resamples, a
random-forest learner (`ranger`, 500 trees, permutation importance), and
model-X equicorrelated knockoff artificial features injected at a 1:1
ratio. At each bootstrap iteration the learner is fit on the real
features plus decoys, and a feature’s per-iteration mask is the set of
penalty thresholds at which its importance beats the FDP+ threshold
calibrated against the decoys. The per-cohort stability score of a
feature is its selection frequency (max-over-lambda) across the 200
bootstraps.

A feature is **consistent** when its minimum score across the eight
cohorts is at least 0.5 (the *min-agg* rule): it must be selected in
more than half of the bootstraps of every cohort. The min-agg rule is
the calibrated aggregation in the WS-5 simulation study (FDP+
0.083–0.142 across shift and drift designs; see
[`?stabl_environment_stability`](https://mdmanurung.github.io/stablr/reference/stabl_environment_stability.md)).

### 3.2 Uncertainty and sign consistency

The per-cohort score is a binomial frequency over 200 bootstraps. For
each feature we report the exact (Clopper–Pearson) 95% interval of the
binding cohort — the cohort that attains the minimum score. This
interval is a conservative uncertainty band for the min-agg score.

Directional replicability is tested with an exact two-sided sign test:
for a feature whose correlation with age shares one sign in k of 8
cohorts, p = 2\sum\_{j=k}^{8}\binom{8}{j}2^{-8}; k = 8 gives p = 0.0078.

### 3.3 Sensitivity analysis

The primary analysis uses the random-forest learner because the WS-2
simulation calibrated it for gaussian problems with up to a few dozen
predictors (mean FDP+ 0.025 linear / 0.065 nonlinear; see
[`?make_ranger_adapter`](https://mdmanurung.github.io/stablr/reference/make_ranger_adapter.md)).
As sensitivity analysis we repeat the full workflow with the lasso
learner, same knockoff type, bootstrap count, and seed. The lasso is
anti-conservative in some regimes (WS-2: FDP+ 0.58 at p = 50), so its
selection is read as a directional check, not a primary result.

### 3.4 Age prediction

We use leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) evaluation. Each fold trains a ridge
regression (`glmnet`, alpha = 0, 5-fold internal CV for the penalty) on
seven cohorts and predicts donor age in the eighth. We evaluate two
models: ridge on all 60 features, and ridge on the 24 consistent
features. We report per-cohort mean absolute error (MAE) with 95%
bootstrap intervals (2,000 donor-level resamples within the held-out
cohort), plus the calibration slope and Pearson correlation of predicted
versus observed age.

All eight directions agree with the source publication: the naive and
recent-thymic-emigrant compartments (CD8+/CD4+ RTE, CD8+ T Naive) and
MAIT fall with age; the terminal-effector compartments (CD4+/CD8+ Temra,
CD8+ Tem GZMK+/GZMB+) rise with age.

Figure 2 shows the per-cohort Spearman correlation of the 24 selected
features. Three observations matter. First, the top rows (CD8+ T Naive,
CD8+/CD4+ RTE, MAIT) are sign-consistent and moderately strong
everywhere (rho between −0.15 and −0.83). Second, the bottom rows pass
the selection bar with weak or cohort-mixed correlations; only 11 of the
24 selected features reach sign-test significance at 0.05, so the panel
separates into a strongly replicable core and a weaker periphery. Third,
SPAC (n = 33) flips the sign of several weak features (for example gd T
Vd1 GZMK+, −0.62 in SPAC against +0.35 in CIMA); small cohorts dominate
the sign pattern of weak rows.

The lasso sensitivity analysis selects 6 features under the same rule

## 5 Result: age prediction on held-out cohorts

Table 2 and Figure 3 give the LOCO results. The all-feature ridge model
predicts age with MAE between 6.7 years (SoundLife) and 15.6 years
(CIMA). The selected-feature model improves two cohorts clearly (CIMA
15.6 to 7.6 years; UCSF 13.4 to 10.8 years), degrades others (OneK1K
15.5 to 17.7 years; SoundLife 6.7 to 9.6 years), and leaves the rest
within overlapping intervals. The overall means (12.1 years all
features, 11.7 years selected) hide this heterogeneity and should not be
read as a uniform improvement.

Figure 4 shows predicted versus observed age for the selected-feature
model. Correlation is satisfactory in every cohort (r between 0.58 and
0.78), but every calibration slope is below 1 (0.30 to 0.75):
predictions shrink toward the training range. The shrinkage is worst in
OneK1K (slope 0.30), the largest cohort with the widest age range, and
mildest in KCL and SoundLife (0.71 to 0.75). The source publication
reports 8.2 years test MAE for its trained clock. Our protocol is
deliberately simpler and different — a ridge model on donor-level
proportions, trained on seven cohorts and applied to the eighth — so the
two numbers are not directly comparable. The honest reading is that
composition features alone carry cross-cohort age signal with good
ranking quality (r up to 0.78) but cohort-specific calibration loss.

## 6 Discussion

Three findings generalize beyond this atlas.

First, the strongly replicable core is small. Five features (CD8+ T
Naive, CD8+ RTE, CD4+ RTE, CD4+ Temra, MAIT) combine near-1 selection
frequencies in every cohort with sign-consistent, moderate correlations.
This matches the publication’s biology: thymic involution and T-cell
compartment restructuring are the most reproducible immune-aging
signatures.

Second, selection frequency and effect size are different quantities.
The min-agg bar asks for replicable *selection*, not for a large or even
sign-stable correlation. Eleven of the 24 selected features have weak or
cohort-mixed correlations. A user who needs a compact panel can read
Figure 2 and take the strongly replicable core; a user who needs
coverage of the full selection set should treat the periphery as
hypotheses.

Third, prediction quality is cohort-specific. The selected panel halves
the error in CIMA and clearly helps UCSF, but hurts OneK1K and
SoundLife. With cohort identity as the largest variance component, no
composition-only model will calibrate uniformly; the LOCO design makes
that cost visible instead of hiding it inside a pooled metric.

## 7 Limits

Four limits bound the conclusions.

1.  **Application case study, not calibration evidence.** The
    calibration evidence for the random-forest learner is
    simulation-based (WS-2) and covers gaussian problems with up to a
    few dozen predictors. This panel has 60 features. The showcase
    demonstrates the workflow on real data; it does not add calibration
    guarantees.
2.  **The min-agg rule is conservative by design.** Features with
    cohort-specific signal fail the bar even when their biology is real.
    CD8+ Tem GZMB+ (score 0.445) is the predeclared example.
3.  **Composition features are relative.** Logit proportions measure a
    cell type against all typed cells; an increase in one compartment
    mechanically dilutes others. Directional claims are about
    composition, not absolute abundance.
4.  **Single run.** The analysis uses one seed (20261013). The bootstrap
    intervals quantify sampling noise within that run; they do not cover
    seed-level variation of the full pipeline.
